**Utilitarian perspective:**

## Overview

This dilemma reveals a fundamental tension between two major ethical traditions:

**Kantian ethics** prioritizes **respecting people's rights and autonomy**, even if doing so produces worse overall outcomes. It says some actions are wrong regardless of their consequences.

**Utilitarian ethics** prioritizes **maximizing overall well-being**, even if this means violating someone's rights or autonomy. It judges actions purely by their outcomes.

In your case, both principles agree the poor's suffering matters morally. They disagree on whether you can violate the rich's rights to help them. Kant says no; utilitarians say yes, as long as the benefits outweigh the costs.
