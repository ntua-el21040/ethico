From a Kantian standpoint, using someone's death—even unintentionally—as a pathway to help others violates their dignity. **The utilitarian perspective** is focused on outcomes: does pressing the button create more overall well-being than not pressing it? ## Overview

This dilemma reveals a deep tension between two major ethical frameworks. Kantian ethics prioritizes respect for individual dignity and consent—it says some actions are wrong even if they produce good outcomes. Utilitarianism is purely outcome-focused and asks only whether more happiness or well-being results overall.

In your case, you're essentially asking whether one innocent person's death can be justified by the greater good. Kantian ethics says "no"—that person's dignity cannot be violated, even for charity. Utilitarianism says "maybe yes"—it depends on the numbers. This is why many real-world moral disagreements exist: we're often implicitly using different ethical frameworks.
